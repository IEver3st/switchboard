Switchboard 0.9.29 fixes microphone cut-outs, audio dropouts under load, and application mixing that could stop working after routing certain game processes.

- Strong noise removal no longer mutes the microphone whenever RNNoise is unsure it hears speech. Between phrases it now lowers residual noise by up to 18 dB instead of silencing it, and it holds open longer across soft syllables and word endings. On a speech-plus-room-noise fixture at strength 85, chopped speech fell from 2.6% to 0.2% of speech frames at 25 dB SNR, and from 3.1% to 1.3% at 12 dB SNR.
- Routed applications, the processed microphone sent to other apps, and microphone monitoring now keep a 20 ms cushion before playback. After a late packet they rebuild that cushion instead of playing a run of tiny gaps. In a simulated 10 s run with up to 5 ms of scheduling jitter, dropouts fell from 5 to 0. Typical added latency is 10 to 20 ms.
- Application mixing no longer shuts down when a routed process has no .exe extension, such as FiveM's browser process. Unreadable recovery entries are skipped, and a corrupt recovery file is set aside rather than disabling mixing. Recovery entries for deleted executables are pruned.
- An app that was mixed in an earlier session is released from the Switchboard cable as soon as it runs again, even when it is no longer mixed. Before, it could keep playing into a cable nothing was forwarding.

Validation covers JavaScript, type and source checks, the Electron build, and the Audio.Host deterministic suite, including new jitter and recovery-journal regressions. A 20 s live run of a HyperX QuadCast 2 through RNNoise into the idle mixer cable had no dropouts. That idle run had no dropouts before this change either, so it does not reproduce the under-load failure. These checks do not establish listening quality in a real call or under game load.
